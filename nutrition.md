## Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)

BMR is the energy your body needs at rest for essential functions such as
breathing, circulating blood, and maintaining body temperature. It excludes
exercise and everyday activity; it is not your daily eating target.

Using the Mifflin–St Jeor equation for men:

```text
BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) + 5
    = (10 × 78) + (6.25 × 175.3) − (5 × 28) + 5
    ≈ 1,740 kcal/day
```

## Estimated maintenance calories

Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) includes BMR and daily activity.

| Activity level | Approximate maintenance calories |
|---|---:|
| Sedentary: mostly sitting | 2,090 kcal/day |
| Lightly active: exercise 1–3 days/week | 2,390 kcal/day |
| Moderately active: exercise 3–5 days/week | 2,700 kcal/day |
| Very active: hard exercise most days | 3,000 kcal/day |

These activity categories are rough estimates. Daily steps, occupation, and
training intensity also affect calorie needs. Refine maintenance using your
average body-weight trend and food intake over 2–3 weeks.

## Macros for building muscle

Muscle growth needs progressive resistance training, enough food, and recovery.
Macro targets support training; they do not build muscle on their own.

- **Protein:** 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day, or approximately **125–172 g/day** at 78 kg.
  A practical target is **160 g/day**, spread across 3–5 meals.
- **Fat:** roughly **20–35% of daily calories**. A practical starting target
  is **70–80 g/day** for the calorie examples below.
- **Carbohydrates:** use the calories remaining after protein and fat.
  They help fuel training; there is no single required amount for muscle growth.

Protein and carbohydrate each provide approximately 4 kcal/g; fat provides
9 kcal/g.

```text
Carbohydrate grams = [daily calories − (protein grams × 4) − (fat grams × 9)] / 4
```

## Starting muscle-gain targets

For gradual muscle gain, start around **150–250 kcal/day above your actual
maintenance**. The examples below use a 200 kcal surplus, 160 g protein,
and 80 g fat. Your activity level has not yet been established.

| Activity level | Daily calories | Protein | Fat | Carbohydrates |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Sedentary | 2,290 kcal | 160 g | 80 g | 233 g |
| Lightly active | 2,590 kcal | 160 g | 80 g | 308 g |
| Moderately active | 2,900 kcal | 160 g | 80 g | 385 g |
| Very active | 3,200 kcal | 160 g | 80 g | 460 g |

Carbohydrates are rounded to the nearest gram. If you train 3–5 days per week
and have moderate overall activity, **2,900 kcal with 160 g protein, 80 g fat,
and 385 g carbohydrates** is a reasonable initial estimate.

## Adjusting the plan

- Weigh yourself under similar conditions and compare weekly averages.
- Aim for gradual gain of approximately **0.1–0.25% of body weight per week**
  (about **0.08–0.20 kg/week** at 78 kg).
- If weight is not increasing after 2–3 weeks, add 100–150 kcal/day.
  If gain is consistently faster than intended, reduce by a similar amount.
- Track strength and waist measurements alongside weight. Beginners can also
  gain muscle at maintenance calories; a surplus is not always necessary.
- Follow a consistent resistance-training program and get 7–9 hours of sleep.
