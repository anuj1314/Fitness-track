# Vegetarian muscle-building diet plan

This plan follows your usual five eating occasions. It excludes curd and yogurt,
includes buttermilk, and uses foods readily available in Bengaluru. It assumes
milk, paneer, and whey are acceptable; eggs are not included.

See [the nutrition guide](nutrition.md) for BMR, metabolism, macro and
micronutrient explanations, and fibre guidance.

## Profile and starting targets

- Male, 28 years; 175.3 cm (5 feet 9 inches); 78 kg.
- IT desk job; running three days/week and gym approximately one hour daily.
- Goal: gradual muscle gain supported by progressive resistance training.
- Start with **2,900–3,100 kcal/day** as a trial, not confirmed maintenance.
- Aim for **150–170 g protein/day** and roughly **75–90 g fat/day**.
- Use carbohydrates for the remaining calories. At 3,000 kcal, an example is
  **160 g protein, 80 g fat, and 410 g carbohydrates**.
- Work toward **30–38 g fibre/day**, increasing gradually as tolerated.

Running distance, gym intensity, steps, and training time have not been provided.
Use your weight trend to refine calories rather than assuming activity labels
are exact.

## Five-meal routine

Protein figures are approximate and depend on brands, recipes, and portions.
Use the actual product labels, especially for tofu, milk, and whey.

| Eating occasion | Suggested meal | Approximate protein |
|---|---|---:|
| Breakfast | 70 g dry oats cooked with 250 ml milk, one scoop whey, and one banana | 38–43 g |
| Snack about 3 hours later | 40 g roasted chana, one fruit, and 15 g almonds | 10–12 g |
| Lunch | 2 chapatis, 1 cup cooked rice, 1 cup cooked dal/rajma/chole, 150 g firm tofu, a serving of vegetables, and a glass of buttermilk | 30–40 g |
| 4 pm snack | One scoop whey with water, 2 slices whole-wheat bread, and one banana | 28–32 g |
| Dinner | Curry made with 50 g dry soy chunks, 3 chapatis, a serving of vegetables, and a glass of buttermilk | 34–40 g |

This template supplies approximately **140–167 g protein/day**; it does not
guarantee a precise calorie or macro total. Measure a few typical days before
assuming it meets the calorie target. Count cooking oil, sauces, and drinks.

### Portion conventions

- Oats, roasted chana, almonds, and soy chunks are weighed **dry/as eaten before
  cooking**. In particular, 50 g dry soy chunks is not 50 g cooked soy chunks.
- Rice, dal, rajma, and chole portions are **cooked**. A cup is approximately
  240 ml; thickness and recipe affect its nutritional value.
- Chapati size varies. For more consistent tracking, use roughly 30 g dry atta
  per chapati and account for any added oil or ghee separately.
- Tofu weight is the drained edible portion. Check the package: some tofu has
  substantially less protein than other firm varieties.
- A scoop of whey means the serving on its label, usually supplying about
  20–25 g protein; scoop weight is not the same as protein weight.
- Use roughly 200–250 ml buttermilk per glass. Dilution changes protein and
  calories considerably.

## Matching the template to your targets

1. Log portions and labels for 3–5 typical days. Compare calories, protein, fat,
   carbohydrates, and fibre with the targets above.
2. If protein is below 150 g, add protein to an existing meal: 250 ml milk
   contributes about 8 g, half a scoop of whey about 10–12 g, and more tofu or
   soy contributes the amount shown on its label. A sixth meal is not required.
3. If calories are low, add measured rice, chapatis, potatoes, or oats. Around
   half a cup of cooked rice provides roughly 100 kcal; a chapati made from
   30 g atta provides roughly 100 kcal before oil or ghee.
4. If fat is low, add measured nuts, seeds, or cooking oil. A teaspoon of oil
   contributes roughly 40 kcal. If fat is high, reduce added oil or use lower-fat
   dairy, keeping adequate protein.
5. Change one portion at a time and reassess rather than adding every option.

## Alternatives for variety

| Meal/component | Alternative | What to account for |
|---|---|---|
| Oats breakfast | 3–4 idlis or 2 dosas with sambar; keep the milk/whey | Idli size, dosa oil, and sambar thickness change calories and protein. |
| Soy dinner | 150–200 g paneer or about 250 g firm tofu | These are not exact macro equivalents. Paneer often adds more fat; tofu protein varies. Prefer lower-fat paneer when useful. |
| Whey | Milk, tofu, paneer, soy chunks, or a suitable plant protein powder | Whey is convenient, not required. Food replacements change calories and fat as well as protein. |
| Lunch legumes | Rotate dal, rajma, chole, and other beans | Increase large legume portions gradually if they cause bloating. |
| Fruit/vegetables | Rotate banana, guava, orange, papaya, seasonal fruit, greens, carrots, beans, capsicum, and gourds | Variety improves micronutrient coverage; vegetables need not appear at every meal. |

**Buttermilk is included for preference and variety, not as a major protein
source.** Do not treat a diluted glass as equivalent to 200 g curd or a
high-protein yogurt serving. Choose a salt level appropriate to the rest of your
diet.

## Running and gym timing

- If training after work, use the 4 pm bread and banana beforehand and take whey
  then or after training, as convenient. There is no need to force a narrow
  post-workout window; daily intake and consistency matter more.
- For morning training, move part of breakfast, such as the banana, before it
  if needed and eat the rest afterward. You can preserve the five-meal pattern.
- Keep large portions of legumes, raw vegetables, and fatty meals away from a
  run if they upset your stomach. Allow more digestion time for larger meals.
- On longer run days, an extra carb portion may be useful if energy, recovery,
  or weight trends indicate a need. Do not automatically add calories already
  included in your daily target. Runs longer than about an hour may need a more
  specific fuelling plan based on duration and intensity.
- Drink regularly and adjust for heat and sweat. Longer, sweaty sessions may
  need electrolytes; buttermilk does not replace an individual hydration plan.

## Recovery and progress checks

- Follow progressive resistance training and get **7–9 hours of sleep**.
- Daily gym attendance does not require daily hard lifting. Include easier
  sessions or rest days if fatigue, soreness, or performance indicate poor recovery.
- Weigh under similar conditions and compare weekly averages, not single days.
- Aim for approximately **0.08–0.20 kg/week** of gradual gain at your current
  weight, alongside improving strength. Monitor waist measurements too.
- If weight stays flat after 2–3 consistent weeks, add **100–150 kcal/day**.
  If it rises consistently faster than intended, reduce by a similar amount.
- Keep protein similar on rest days. Beginners may gain muscle at maintenance;
  increasing calories is not compulsory if strength and body composition improve.

## Practical preparation

Keep oats, atta, rice, roasted chana, soy chunks, nuts, and your chosen protein
powder available. Rotate legumes and buy milk, tofu/paneer, fruit, vegetables,
and buttermilk as needed. Prepare legumes and vegetables ahead to simplify office
lunches. Refrigerate perishable meals promptly and keep dairy/tofu lunches cold
during transport or at the office.

Review the vegetarian micronutrient section in [nutrition.md](nutrition.md),
especially vitamin B12, calcium, iron, and vitamin D. Hitting protein and calorie
targets alone does not establish a nutritionally complete diet.
